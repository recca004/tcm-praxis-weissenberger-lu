# Photography Brief — TCM Praxis Weissenberger-Lu

**For:** Min Weissenberger-Lu
**Purpose:** a small set of real photographs to replace the labeled placeholders on the new website.
**Tone:** calm, warm, honest — the feeling of sitting in the consultation room. No stock-photo gloss, no staged "wellness" poses, no white-coat clinical look.

We need **6–8 photographs**. A good phone camera in daylight is enough — what matters is light, calm, and tidiness. If you prefer, a half-hour with a local photographer covering the list below is also fine.

---

## Shot list

### 1. Practitioner portrait (the most important one)
- **Used on:** homepage "Your Practitioner" section and the About page portrait slot.
- **Framing:** vertical (portrait), waist-up or 3/4 length. You standing or seated in the practice, looking at the camera or slightly past it, natural relaxed expression — the way you'd greet a patient.
- **Setting:** in the treatment room or near the window, softly out-of-focus background.
- **Crop delivered:** portrait orientation, roughly 4:5. Leave some space above the head.

### 2. The practice room
- **Used on:** hero image (top of homepage), possibly Treatments page.
- **Framing:** landscape or portrait of the treatment table in natural light — the room as a patient first sees it. Tidy, warm, lived-in rather than showroom-perfect.
- **Crop delivered:** both a landscape (16:10-ish) and a portrait (4:5) version if possible — the homepage hero currently uses portrait.

### 3. Consultation setting
- **Used on:** First Visit page / treatment explainer sections.
- **Framing:** the conversation moment — two chairs, the desk, pulse-taking or talking hands (a patient model/friend's hands are fine; faces optional). Should feel unhurried.
- **Note:** if a patient appears, ask their permission (a hand or shoulder from behind avoids the issue entirely).

### 4. Treatment preparation
- **Used on:** Treatments page detail sections.
- **Framing:** close detail — needles laid out, herbal drawer/jars, a cupping set, folded towels, hands preparing a treatment. 2–3 detail shots are welcome.
- **Light:** near a window, side light; avoid flash on reflective jars.

### 5. Interior details
- **Used on:** About page, section backgrounds where needed.
- **Framing:** small vignettes — the diploma/certificates wall, a plant, the waiting chair, the apothecary shelf. 1–2 shots.

### 6. Entrance / location
- **Used on:** Contact page (helps patients find the door).
- **Framing:** the building entrance at Schaffhauserstrasse 466, ideally with the practice sign visible, and one wider street view toward Seebacherplatz so visitors recognise the corner.

---

## Technical guidance

- **Light:** photograph in daylight, blinds open, ideally late morning. Avoid mixed daylight + ceiling lamps if possible — pick one light source.
- **Orientation:** deliver both portrait and landscape where noted; the site crops to 4:5 (portrait), 16:10 and 4:3 (landscape).
- **Resolution:** native phone resolution is fine (≥2000 px on the long edge). No filters, no HDR halos, mild edits only.
- **Background:** tidy surfaces, personal touches welcome (books, herbs, certificates) — they make it yours, not a template.
- **What NOT to shoot:** no staged smiling-at-camera "team" poses, no syringe-style clinical close-ups, nothing with visible patient records or names.

## How the photos will be used

- Until each photo is delivered and approved by you, the site keeps its **labeled placeholders** ("Beispielfoto vor Freigabe" / portrait silhouette). Nothing is published as your practice until you sign it off.
- Please send originals via WhatsApp or email; Mario will handle cropping, compression (WebP) and alt text in all three languages.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Portrait: recognisable, relaxed, in-practice background — you approve it.
- [ ] Room shot: warm daylight, table visible, calm atmosphere.
- [ ] 2–3 detail shots: needles/herbs/cupping/hands.
- [ ] Entrance shot with recognisable surroundings (Seebacherplatz side).
- [ ] All delivered in original resolution.
- [ ] Min's written approval (WhatsApp is fine) before any photo goes live.
